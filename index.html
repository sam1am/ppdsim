How this works

From the diagonal and resolution the page works out the size of one of your pixels, and from your viewing distance the angle that pixel covers. That gives your display's native PPD. To simulate a lower PPD, each sample is rendered onto a coarser pixel grid and every one of those coarse pixels is then drawn as a block of your real pixels, so it covers exactly the angle it would on the simulated display. Everything keeps the same physical size on your screen; only the pixel density changes.

  • You can only simulate downwards. A display can't show more detail than it has. To preview a higher PPD, move back from the screen and update the viewing distance.
  • Keep browser zoom at 100% and enter the panel's native resolution, not a scaled "looks like" one. Stay at the distance you entered: the simulation is only right from there.
  • Text is antialiased in greyscale. Real low-density displays often use subpixel rendering and hinting, which can make small text a little crisper than shown here.
  • PPD is measured at the centre of the screen. On a flat panel it rises slightly towards the edges. Headset figures are approximate centre values and ignore lens blur.
